## Usefulness judge

Qwen3-8B scores each answer 1–5 against the reference answer with a fixed rubric (`JUDGE_RUBRIC` in `scripts/benchmark.py`); usefulness = share of answers scoring 4 or 5. The judge is the same small model as the copilot, so its number is reported only with its agreement with people on 20 answers (`reports/human_check.csv`).

## Team notes

We ran three dev runs. Runs 2 and 3 used fresh model calls through their own cache salts.

Variance. All quality metrics were identical across runs (SD 0): accuracy 1.0, completion 100%, fact recall 1.0, checklist accuracy 1.0, score reproducibility 1.0. Only latency varied (p50 5.9–6.6 s, p95 12.6–14.5 s). At temperature 0 the hosted Qwen3-8B returned the same replies on repeat calls, and the band, score, checklist and approvers come from deterministic tools (D2, D24). So results are reproducible on dev. Sampling noise doesn’t explain the ablation differences, but with 30 queries, one query (3.3 points) remains the smallest difference we can measure.
Targets. Six of seven targets are met: accuracy, completion, checklist accuracy, score reproducibility, usefulness (0.96 of answers scored ≥ 4 by the judge), and refusals for approval, signing and payment requests.
Citation precision: 0.807 → 0.927 after D43 (target 0.90). Hit rate was already 1.0. The whole shortfall came from extra citations on 6 of 16 dev question answers, and most of those came from our own key-fact lines. “Missing” verdicts listed the documents the checklist had examined, and single-fact contract questions picked up a policy document. D43 limits each line to documents that support its verdict and removes internal section names from answers in code. The first version also added a prompt rule. It met the precision target only partly (0.844) and moved three unrelated answers, taking accuracy to 0.967, so we removed it. Lesson: at temperature 0 the same prompt gives the same answer, but rewording a prompt shared by every question still changes one or two answers. Changes are therefore kept to the prompts they target. The remaining gap is mostly EVAL-028, which cites the DPA as well as the contract. The DPA does state the 72-hour term, so the expected-documents list is narrower than the evidence.
Scorer limitation. Fact recall matches exact substrings. In the rejected run, a correct answer that wrote “emergencies” missed the expected fact “emergency” (EVAL-059, judge 5/5). eval_v1 is frozen, so we left the scorer unchanged. A stem- or synonym-aware match is future work.
Which run is which. The three-run variance result (SD 0) and the judge’s human calibration were measured before D43. The post-D43 figures come from one run, which is justified because the spread was zero. reports/benchmark_v1_preD43.json keeps the earlier runs. The ablation table’s full-agent row is also from before D43.
Judge calibration. See above: 95% exact agreement on 20 blind human scores. Kappa is 0.0 only because humans rated every answer useful.
Limitation. The dev split is at its ceiling, so these numbers show the agent works reliably on dev questions but not how far ahead of a weaker design it is. The test split (0.82 at the Week 3 gate) remains the honest held-out figure and was not rerun (D12).
